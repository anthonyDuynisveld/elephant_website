Elephants in Popular Culture

From beloved animated characters to powerful documentary subjects, elephants have captured human imagination for centuries. Here's how they appear across movies, books, documentaries, and more.

Movies

Elephants on the Big Screen

Dumbo (1941 & 2019)

Disney's classic tale of a baby circus elephant with oversized ears who learns to fly. The original animated film and Tim Burton's live-action remake both became cultural touchstones, teaching children about being different and finding your strengths.

Disney's Dumbo →

The Jungle Book (1967 & 2016)

Colonel Hathi and his elephant patrol march through the jungle in this adaptation of Rudyard Kipling's story. The elephants are portrayed as disciplined, military-like creatures — a mix of humor and respect.

Disney's Jungle Book →

Tarzan (1999)

Disney's Tarzan features Tantor, a lovable but anxious elephant who becomes one of Tarzan's closest friends. Tantor shows elephants as loyal, emotional, and surprisingly brave when their friends need help.

Disney's Tarzan →

Operation Dumbo Drop (1995)

Based on a true story from the Vietnam War, this film follows soldiers who must transport an elephant through the jungle. It highlights the cultural importance elephants hold in many Southeast Asian communities.

Books

Elephants in Literature

Babar the Elephant

Created by Jean de Brunhoff in 1931, Babar is a young elephant who leaves the jungle, visits a city, and returns to become king of the elephants. The series has been translated into dozens of languages and remains a children's classic.

View on Amazon →

The Elephant Whisperer

Lawrence Anthony's true story about adopting a herd of wild elephants on his game reserve in South Africa. The book shows the deep emotional bonds that can form between humans and elephants.

View on Amazon →

Horton Hears a Who!

Dr. Seuss's beloved story about an elephant who discovers an entire tiny civilization living on a speck of dust. Horton's famous line — "A person's a person, no matter how small" — has become a universal message about caring for others.

View on Amazon →

The One and Only Ivan

Katherine Applegate's award-winning novel tells the story of a gorilla and his elephant friend Stella in a mall zoo. The book highlights how elephants suffer in captivity and inspired real-world conservation action.

View on Amazon →

This PBS documentary follows Echo, the matriarch of an elephant family in Kenya's Amboseli National Park, over 30 years. It reveals the complex social lives, grief, and intelligence of wild elephants.

Misrepresentation

When Popular Culture Gets It Wrong

While elephants are often portrayed positively, some popular culture representations can be misleading or harmful:

Circus Performances

For over a century, elephants were stars of circus shows — standing on their hind legs, balancing on balls, and performing tricks. This gave audiences the impression that elephants enjoy performing. In reality, circus training often involved harsh methods. Many countries and circuses have now banned elephant performances.

"Elephants Are Afraid of Mice"

This myth has appeared in cartoons and stories for generations. There is no scientific evidence that elephants fear mice. The myth likely started because elephants are cautious around small, fast-moving animals they can't see clearly — but fear of mice specifically is fiction.

"Elephants Never Forget"

While elephants do have extraordinary memories — remembering water sources, migration routes, and individual elephants over decades — the saying oversimplifies their intelligence. Elephants don't remember everything perfectly. What they do have is remarkable long-term spatial and social memory, which is different from total recall.

Why it matters: When movies and stories show elephants doing tricks happily or portray them as simple, funny animals, it can make people forget that elephants are wild animals with complex needs. Misrepresentation can reduce public support for conservation and make it harder to protect elephants in the wild.
