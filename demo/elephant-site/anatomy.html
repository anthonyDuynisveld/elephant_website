Animal Anatomy and Biology

The African elephant possesses some of the most remarkable anatomical features in the animal kingdom. From their prehensile trunks to their massive ears, every aspect of their body has evolved to suit their environment and lifestyle.

Size Information

  • Height Adult males (bulls): 3.2 to 4.0 meters (10.5 to 13 feet) at the shoulder. Adult females (cows): 2.2 to 2.8 meters (7.2 to 9.2 feet).
  • Weight Adult males: 4,500 to 6,000 kg (10,000 to 13,200 pounds). Adult females: 2,700 to 3,600 kg (6,000 to 8,000 pounds). The heaviest recorded bull weighed approximately 10,400 kg.
  • Body Length 5.5 to 7.5 meters (18 to 24.6 feet) from trunk to tail.

Lifespan

African elephants live an average of 60 to 70 years in the wild. Some individuals have been known to reach 80 years of age. Their longevity is closely tied to their teeth: elephants cycle through six sets of molars during their lifetime. When the final set wears down and the animal can no longer chew food effectively, it will eventually die of malnutrition.

How They Breathe

Elephants breathe through their trunk and mouth. The trunk, which contains over 40,000 muscles, acts as both a nose and an upper lip. Elephants can also use their trunk as a snorkel when swimming through deep water, holding it above the surface to breathe while the rest of their body is submerged. Their large lungs are attached directly to the chest wall and diaphragm rather than hanging freely, which is an adaptation to the pressure differences their bodies experience.

Unique Physical Characteristics

The Trunk

The trunk is a fusion of the nose and upper lip, containing over 40,000 muscles with no bones. It can lift up to 300 kg, yet is dexterous enough to pick up a single blade of grass. Elephants use it for breathing, smelling, drinking, touching, grabbing, and producing sound.

Ears

African elephant ears can reach 1.8 meters (6 feet) in length. They are packed with blood vessels and function as radiators, dissipating heat to help regulate body temperature. Ear flapping increases air flow over the blood vessels, cooling the blood by up to 5 degrees Celsius.

Tusks

Tusks are elongated incisor teeth made of ivory (dentine). They grow throughout the elephant's life and can reach over 3 meters in length, weighing up to 100 kg each. Elephants can be left-tusked or right-tusked, similar to human handedness.

Feet

Elephant feet have a spongy pad of tissue beneath the bones that acts as a shock absorber. This padding allows them to walk almost silently despite their massive weight. Each foot has five toes, though not all have visible toenails.

Skin

Elephant skin is about 2.5 cm (1 inch) thick but is highly sensitive. The wrinkled texture of their skin helps retain moisture and mud, which protects against sunburn and parasites. Despite its thickness, it is vulnerable to insect bites.

Brain

The elephant brain weighs approximately 5 kg (11 pounds), the largest of any land animal. They possess a highly developed hippocampus, which is associated with memory and emotion. Elephants demonstrate self-awareness, grief, empathy, and problem-solving abilities.
