Animal Behavior

African elephants are among the most intelligent and socially complex animals on Earth. Their behavior reveals deep emotional capacity, sophisticated communication, and survival strategies refined over millions of years of evolution.

How They Get Food

Elephants are resourceful foragers that use their trunks, tusks, and feet to access food. They grasp branches with their trunk, strip bark from trees with their tusks, and kick at roots to loosen them from the ground. During the dry season, elephants dig wells in dry riverbeds using their tusks and front feet, reaching underground water that other animals then also use.

Matriarchs use their decades of experience and spatial memory to lead herds to food and water sources that may be hundreds of kilometers away. This knowledge, passed from generation to generation, is essential for the herd's survival during droughts and periods of scarcity.

Defense Against Predators

Adult elephants have very few natural predators due to their massive size. Lions and crocodiles occasionally target young calves, but the herd provides strong protection. When threatened, elephants form a defensive circle around the calves, with adults facing outward.

A charging elephant can reach speeds of up to 40 km/h (25 mph). They use their tusks, trunk, and sheer body weight as weapons. Elephants also employ threat displays, including ear spreading to appear larger, loud trumpeting, ground stamping, and mock charges to intimidate potential threats before resorting to physical confrontation.

Sleeping Habits

African elephants are among the shortest-sleeping mammals, averaging only 2 to 4 hours of sleep per day. They sleep both standing and lying down, though they lie down only when they feel safe, typically in the middle of the night. Standing sleep occurs in brief naps throughout the day and night.

Elephants experience REM (rapid eye movement) sleep only when lying on their sides, which may occur as infrequently as every 3 to 4 days. During periods of perceived danger, such as the presence of predators or poachers, elephants may go without lying down to sleep for several days, covering long distances instead.

Other Interesting Behaviors

Communication

Elephants communicate using a range of sounds including trumpets, rumbles, roars, and barks. Many of their vocalizations are infrasonic, below the range of human hearing, and can travel through the ground for up to 10 kilometers. They also communicate through touch, body language, and chemical signals.

Grief and Mourning

Elephants are one of the few species known to mourn their dead. They have been observed gently touching the bones and tusks of deceased elephants with their trunks, standing silently over the remains for extended periods, and returning to the site of a death repeatedly over months or years.

Play

Young elephants engage in play behaviors including chasing, wrestling, trunk sparring, and sliding down muddy banks. Play is important for developing social bonds and physical coordination. Adults also play, particularly during and after bathing.

Tool Use

Elephants use branches to swat flies, scratch themselves with sticks, and drop logs or rocks on electric fences to break them. They have been observed plugging water holes they have dug with chewed bark to prevent evaporation and return later to drink.
