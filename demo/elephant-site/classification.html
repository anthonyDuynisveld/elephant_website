Scientific Name and Classification

The African elephant belongs to the genus Loxodonta, which is derived from the Greek words loxo (oblique) and donta (tooth), referring to the distinctive lozenge-shaped ridges on their molar teeth. African elephants are classified within the order Proboscidea, which historically included mammoths and mastodons.

Primary Species

African Bush Elephant

Loxodonta africana

The larger of the two species, the bush elephant is the largest living land animal. Males can stand up to 4 meters tall and weigh up to 6,000 kg. They inhabit savannas, grasslands, and woodlands across eastern and southern Africa. Their ears are large and shaped roughly like the African continent.

African Forest Elephant

Loxodonta cyclotis

Smaller than its bush cousin, the forest elephant stands up to 2.5 meters tall and weighs up to 2,700 kg. They live in the dense tropical rainforests of central and western Africa. Their tusks are straighter and point downward, adapted for navigating dense forest, and their ears are more rounded and smaller.

Additional Classification Details

Until 2010, the African bush elephant and African forest elephant were considered subspecies of a single species. Genetic analysis revealed that the two diverged between 2 and 7 million years ago, making them as genetically distinct from each other as Asian elephants are from woolly mammoths. The IUCN now recognizes them as separate species.

The African bush elephant is currently listed as Endangered, while the African forest elephant is listed as Critically Endangered on the IUCN Red List. Both species face threats from poaching for ivory and habitat loss due to human expansion.

A third species, the West African elephant, has been proposed by some researchers based on genetic and morphological differences among populations in West Africa, but this classification is not yet widely accepted.
