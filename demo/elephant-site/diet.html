Diet Information

African elephants are herbivores with enormous appetites. They spend up to 16 hours a day eating and consume between 150 and 300 kilograms (330 to 660 pounds) of vegetation daily. Their diet varies by season, habitat, and availability, but they are highly adaptable and eat a wide range of plant material.

What They Eat

  • Grasses The primary food source, especially during the wet season when fresh grass is abundant. Elephants use their trunks to pull up large clumps of grass and shake off the soil before eating.
  • Tree Bark Elephants strip bark from trees using their tusks and trunk. Bark provides roughage, fiber, and essential minerals like calcium.
  • Leaves & Branches They reach high into trees with their trunks or push entire trees over to access foliage. Leaves from acacia and baobab trees are favorites.
  • Fruits When available, elephants eagerly seek out wild fruits such as marula, figs, and berries. They play a critical role in seed dispersal for many tree species.
  • Roots & Tubers During the dry season, elephants dig into the ground with their tusks and feet to access roots and tubers that hold moisture and nutrients.
  • Shrubs & Herbs Various shrubs, herbs, and creepers make up a significant portion of the diet, especially in forest habitats.

How Often and How Much

Elephants eat almost constantly throughout the day and night. They typically feed for 12 to 16 hours each day. An adult bull elephant can consume up to 300 kg of food daily, while females typically consume around 150 to 200 kg.

They drink 80 to 200 liters (20 to 50 gallons) of water per day. In extremely hot conditions, this amount can increase significantly. Their trunk can hold up to 8 liters of water at a time, which they squirt into their mouths.

Despite the enormous volume of food they consume, elephants digest only about 40% of what they eat. This low digestive efficiency means they must eat large quantities to obtain enough nutrients.
